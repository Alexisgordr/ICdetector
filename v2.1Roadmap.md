# ICdetection — Roadmap

**Current version:** v2.1 (`versionCode = 3`) — released.
**Next:** v2.2, after a second field-collection phase.

The first collection phase produced 1,065 records over 59 days (173 cells). Its most valuable result was not a detection: it was discovering that the collection itself was broken in four ways, so it could not answer the questions it had been designed to answer. v2.1 fixes those. The full evidence and reasoning is in `CHANGELOG-v2.1.md`.

The remaining improvement margin is still not a magic new signal against sophisticated IMSI-catchers. ICdetection is limited by Android userland: it cannot inspect RRC/NAS/baseband traffic like dedicated hardware tools can. The realistic goal stays the same — improve internal coherence, reduce false positives, and make better use of local history.

---

## ⚠️ Installing v2.1 — uninstall first

**v2.1 must be installed as a fresh install. Uninstall the previous version, then install v2.1.** An in-place update will be refused by Android.

Two independent reasons:

1. **Signature.** If the v2.1 APK is not signed with the exact keystore that signed the copy already on the device, Android refuses the update (`INSTALL_FAILED_UPDATE_INCOMPATIBLE`). A rebuilt APK signed with a different key — including a fresh debug key — always hits this.
2. **Clean history, which you want anyway.** Rows written before v2.1 may hold an antenna coordinate from the verification APIs in `lat`/`lon`, where the device's own GPS position belongs. The v9 migration deliberately does not rewrite them: after the fact there is no reliable way to tell which is which. Uninstalling clears the database and preferences, so the new baseline is built only from correct data.

Nothing is lost that matters — **except the history itself**. If you want to keep the old records for analysis, export the CSV *before* uninstalling. Bear in mind that in that export `Lat`/`Lon` may be either your position or the antenna's, with no way to distinguish them; that ambiguity is exactly what v2.1 ends.

*Note for future releases: once v2.1 is installed, later versions signed with the same keystore will update in place normally. This is a one-time step.*

## ✅ Completed in v2.1

Everything here is backed by a measurement from the field data, not by speculation.

### Data integrity

- **The forensic history no longer erases the reason for sub-threshold anomalies.** 33 of the 34 penalised rows in two months recorded `FailedHeuristics = OK` while carrying a real score of 85 or 75 — an internal contradiction. `applyTemporalConfidence()` was setting `suspiciousReason = null` whenever the cell did not reach the alarm threshold. The reason is now kept, tagged `[sub-umbral]`. Alarm behaviour is unchanged: nothing sounds, nothing is shown as a threat, and the 3-cycle confirmation gate is untouched.
- **API tower coordinates no longer overwrite GPS positions.** The recurring ~1,100 km coordinate never came from the GPS — which is why none of v2.0's GPS hardening could catch it. `updateVerificationStatus()` was writing the coordinate returned by WiGLE/OpenCellID into `lat`/`lon` of every row of that cell still `PENDING` — precisely the rows that had no GPS fix. New columns `api_lat`/`api_lon` hold it now; **`lat`/`lon` is written by the GPS and by nothing else**.
- **API responses are validated.** Three barriers: a sentinel detector (a coordinate already on record for ≥ 3 distinct Cell IDs is an API default, not an antenna — needs no GPS); the 50 km check against a live fix; and, with no live fix, 500 km against the last accepted position. A rejected coordinate no longer yields `VERIFIED` — it is treated as `NOT_FOUND` and retried in 1 h, closing a false-negative pathway worth +15 score.
- **The audit log is consistent with it.** A cell with failed heuristics below the threshold no longer reports "✅ SEGURO".

### Heuristics — false-positive control

- **Tier 1 #1 — full identity in temporal-confirmation keys.** The streak map keys on `MCC-MNC-TAC-CID`. Last place still using a bare `cellId`.
- **Tier 2 #6 — H15 PCI logic matured, answered with data.** v2.0 removed ARFCN from this heuristic because carrier aggregation makes it flap benignly, and kept PCI on the premise that a legitimate cell never flaps its PCI. Longer data falsified that premise: 26 of 173 cells showed multiple PCI values, and the PCI × ARFCN cross-tabulation was perfectly correlated — the modem attributes the secondary carrier's PCI to the serving cell exactly as it does its ARFCN. H15 now compares PCI **only within the same carrier**. Simulated over the 59 days: one cell fired before, none fires now, and a genuine same-carrier flap still fires at full penalty.
- **H14 band downgrade — two necessary conditions added.** 12 of the 34 deductions were high-band → sub-GHz transitions with the *new* cell at −101 to −116 dBm: ordinary handovers to the coverage layer, not forced downgrades. `prevStrong` only looked at the previous cell. Now the new low-band cell must read ≥ −95 dBm and must not be weaker than the one you had — both necessary conditions of the attack model, so no real signature is lost. The progressive-degradation exception (garage/basement) is unchanged, and its cross-handover trend buffer is deliberately **not** reset: it has to span the handover to work.

### Data volume

- **Periodic sampling of the serving cell.** Median was **2 samples per cell**; 38 % of cells seen exactly once; only 6 of 173 reached the 30 samples the RSRQ/SINR fingerprint needs — it had been dormant for the entire period and would have stayed dormant. Eight hours camped on one cell produced zero samples, because history was only written on handover. Now a sample is recorded roughly every 5 min with the screen on and every 15 min with it off. It does **not** wake the GPS and does **not** force extra radio reads: it persists the analysis the service loop already performs. Cost ≈ 150 rows/day, ≈ 9,000 over the 60-day retention window.
- **Three adjustments so that more data does not degrade the heuristics.** `getPreviousCellHistory()` reads up to 300 rows and trims to 20 *after* the 50 m distance filter (with the old `LIMIT 20`, same-place samples would have consumed the whole quota and left H11 with nothing usable). `getCellRfFingerprint()` confines samples to a ~1 km zone, keeping rows without coordinates so indoor volume is not lost. H15's per-carrier shares become more robust as counts rise.

### Export and plumbing

- **`ThreatProb` persisted and exported.** The Bayesian posterior was computed every cycle and thrown away. Item #7 below asks for recalibrating those likelihood ratios against field data; without the model's output in the export there was nothing to calibrate.
- **`ApiLat` / `ApiLon` exported**, so the provenance of every coordinate is visible.
- **Database schema v8 → v9**, three additive `ALTER TABLE` statements, individually guarded. No row is modified, moved or deleted.
- **History screen** renders sub-threshold observations in grey as `· reason`, distinct from a red `⚠️ Fallo:` alarm, and excludes them from the "anomalous cells" counter — they are analysis material, not incidents.
- **Housekeeping:** WiGLE `User-Agent` updated to `ICdetection/2.1`.
- **Tests:** `BandDowngradeTest` updated to the new H14 contract with three cases added, including the real −85 → −108 dBm handover. `HeuristicsTest` keeps its H15 cases (now exercising the compatibility path) and adds four for the per-carrier rule, including the real cell in both directions.

## Still open — v2.2

These were data-dependent and the first collection could not feed them. With v2.1's logging in place, a second 2–3 month phase should.

### Tier 1 — Low risk, high value

#### 2. History-Maturity Confidence Tiers

The app already has a trust/reputation concept. Exposing maturity tiers would make it easier to interpret.

**Goal:** avoid treating a cell seen 4 times like a cell seen hundreds of times across many days.

**Revision after the field data.** The originally proposed tiers were calendar-based (`<7 days`, `7–30`, `30–90`, `90+`). Calendar age is the wrong axis: with the v2.0 sampling rate a "90-day" cell could have 3 samples, so the tier would have been lying. Tiers must be defined by **sample count and number of distinct days with observations**. v2.1's periodic sampling makes those numbers meaningful for the first time.

**Expected effect:** better user interpretation and more conservative trust handling.

#### 3. Advanced Modem Security Callbacks

The UI and audit log treat hardware ciphering as `N/A` when Android/device APIs do not expose the signal. ICdetection does not claim direct null-cipher or IMSI-disclosure detection on standard no-root installs. A placeholder callback path is kept but generates no alerts and does not affect scoring.

**Goal:** implement real `SecurityAlgorithmsListener` / `CellularIdentifierDisclosedListener` support only when Android 16+ hardware and permissions can be tested properly. These APIs likely require `READ_PRIVILEGED_PHONE_STATE`, which a sideloaded app cannot hold — so this may remain infeasible on normal installs, and Android 16 already rejects null ciphers at OS level.

**Expected effect:** future modem-security visibility where the OS actually exposes it. No current detection impact.

### Tier 2 — Valuable, data-dependent

#### 5. Robust Baseline for H13

H13 uses mean/stddev plus percentiles. Mean/stddev can be skewed by outliers.

**Goal:** evaluate median, MAD, and stronger percentile logic once enough field data exists.

**Partially addressed in v2.1:** the "separate samples by approximate zone" idea was applied to the RSRQ/SINR fingerprint, because the periodic sampling made it necessary there. The same treatment for the RSRP baseline is still open, as is the median/MAD work — and it is now feasible, since the baseline will have real sample counts.

#### 7. Bayesian Weight Recalibration

The current likelihood ratios are reasoned estimates, not measured constants.

**Goal:** review which heuristics fire often, which normalise quickly, and which persist.

**Unblocked in v2.1.** This was impossible before: the reason for every sub-threshold failure was erased at write time and the posterior was never persisted. Both are now in the export (`FailedHeuristics` with the `[sub-umbral]` prefix, and `ThreatProb`).

**Expected effect:** reduce overweighted noisy signals, preserve strong physical/contextual indicators.

#### 8. Smarter Reputation Decay

A cell trusted months ago should not keep full confidence forever if it disappears and later returns.

**Goal:** a simple time-decay model — recent clean history = high trust; old inactive history = gradually reduced; returning cell = remembered, not blindly trusted.

**Expected effect:** better long-term behaviour without discarding useful history.

#### 9. CSV Import / Restore

Export exists; import would turn it into a real backup system.

**Goal:** import only with strong safety rules — parse the same columns `ExportUtils` writes (including the three added in v2.1), support RFC 4180 escaping, validate every row before insertion, use a transaction, deduplicate, offer a dry-run summary, and reject malformed rows instead of guessing.

**Risk note:** this writes to the database. Additive, but not zero-risk.

### Tier 3 — High value, high effort / risk

#### 10. Neighbor Fingerprint

A false base station may clone the serving cell but not reproduce the surrounding RF ecosystem.

**Goal:** learn historical neighbour snapshots per cell/zone — neighbour PCI/ARFCN, approximate relative powers, neighbour count, repeated local patterns — then flag major mismatches conservatively.

**Risk:** high false-positive potential; neighbour sets change legitimately and often. **Precondition:** do not attempt before the sample counts from v2.1's periodic logging are in. With a median of 2 samples per cell there was no basis for this whatsoever.

#### 11. Combination Scoring

The Bayesian scorer already combines evidence and groups correlated heuristics. A future layer could detect persistent multi-signal combinations.

**Risk:** if naive, it amplifies false positives. It creates no new signal against sophisticated catchers; it only improves interpretation where Android-visible traces already exist. Should follow #7, not precede it.

#### 12. Transition Graph

Track normal transitions between cells (`A -> B -> C`) and flag unusual ones only under strong gating: low speed, strong signal, repeated short-window transition, suspicious neighbour context.

**Risk:** very high false-positive potential. New routes, temporary coverage changes and normal handovers all look unusual. Only consider after neighbour fingerprinting proves useful.

#### 13. Forensic Capture During Ping-Pong (without relaxing confirmation)

A rapid ping-pong between a suspicious cell and a legitimate one resets the per-cell streak on each bounce, so the suspicious cell never reaches 3-cycle confirmation and writes no forensic row (the ping-pong itself is still alerted). Relaxing confirmation would catch it at the cost of more false positives, against the project's philosophy.

**Goal:** capture evidence of the suspicious observations during a ping-pong event, linked to the existing ping-pong alert, **without** changing the confirmation logic.

**Note:** v2.1's periodic sampling partially mitigates this by accident — a ping-pong lasting longer than the sampling interval now leaves rows behind, including the sub-threshold reason. That is not a substitute for a deliberate design, but it means the gap is no longer total.

## A methodological note from v2.1

Two of the four main v2.1 findings were bugs inside fixes that v2.0 had already declared closed and "field-confirmed" — and both were visible within minutes of opening the exported CSV alongside the code.

The lesson is not that something was missed. It is that **field validation needs an automatic check, not a careful read.** A test that opens an export and verifies a handful of invariants — no row with `score < 100` and reason `OK`; no coordinate repeated identically across more than two cells; no implied speed above 400 km/h between consecutive rows — would have caught both on day one. That is worth more than any new heuristic, and it is the first thing to build for v2.2.

## Not Planned

- **Using ARFCN as an identity signal in H15.** Field data showed ARFCN fluctuates legitimately due to carrier aggregation. (v2.1 note: the same turned out to be true of PCI *across* carriers, which is why H15 now compares within a carrier.)
- **Adding more instant RF/power/neighbour rules without data.** Likely to increase false positives.
- **Enabling ProGuard/R8 minification.** Low value for a GPL open-source project, with silent-breakage risk.
- **Waking GPS on every cell change while the screen is off.** Would harm battery life and goes against the low-drain design. (v2.1's periodic sampling deliberately does **not** do this: it uses whatever fix already exists and leaves the coordinate null otherwise.)
- **Holding a permanent `PARTIAL_WAKE_LOCK`.** Would destroy battery life and make multi-month collection unusable.
- **Advertising or tracking monetization.** Against the privacy-first philosophy of the project.

---

## Long-Term Vision

A possible future direction is a privacy-preserving community anomaly database: users voluntarily sharing anonymised, aggregated data to build known-legitimate tower baselines per city or region.

Hard requirements before this is even considered: no user movement traces; no raw GPS history sharing; aggregation to tower/area level only; anti-poisoning protections; transparent opt-in design; local-first behaviour preserved.

This is not a v2.2 feature. It would be a separate long-term project.

## Already shipped in v2.0 (reference)

Kept for the record so these are not re-done. All of this predates v2.1.

- Database indexes on `(cid, mnc, tac, mcc)` and `(timestamp)`.
- `getRecords()` export wrapped in `try/catch` so malformed rows cannot crash history export.
- Coordinate backfill scoped only to the current observation (`updateNullCoordinates`).
- CSV field escaping according to RFC 4180.
- Ciphering/IMSI wording corrected: unavailable modem-security signals show as `N/A`. The old dead callback methods were removed.
- Persist alarm evidence to history, gated to the temporally-confirmed path so the forensic log contains 3-cycle-confirmed alarms only.
- `isPlausibleFix()`: reject GPS fixes implying > 400 km/h, guarding all three GPS→DB paths, with self-healing.
- GPS-only location: no `NETWORK_PROVIDER` subscription and no `getLastKnownLocation(NETWORK_PROVIDER)`.
- Persist the GPS plausibility reference across restarts, with a 6 h staleness guard.
- GPS-to-cell race fix: the active cell is re-captured *after* the 3 s stabilisation delay.
- Full cell identity (`+ MCC`) in `getPreviousCellHistory`, `getCellSignalBaseline`, `getCellReputation`, `getCellRfFingerprint` and the prefetch cache signature.
- Latency indicator honesty: initialises to `N/A`, reports `N/A` under WiFi/VPN/Tor, three UI states, and the Settings toggle is greyed out while a VPN is active.
- Telephony callback watchdog based on a real liveness timestamp instead of a latching boolean.
- H8/H3 false-positive fixes: LTE EARFCN 0 is valid (Band 1); H3 does not flag MCC inconsistency while the serving MCC is still `"N/A"`.
- Active cell protected from cache eviction in `pruneCaches()`.
- Crash hardening: `startForeground()` in `try/catch`, `CoroutineExceptionHandler` on the service scope, guarded bind in `MainActivity`.
- Notification repaint throttled to once per 2 s (DB logging, audit logs and alarm tone stay real-time).
- Apply known verification status *before* threat analysis, so the recorded score reflects verification in the same cycle.

*Note on two of these: the alarm-evidence fix and the GPS-poison fixes were marked "field-confirmed" in v2.0. The v2.1 analysis showed both were incomplete — the reason was still being erased for sub-threshold rows, and the ~1,100 km coordinate was entering through the API path, not the GPS. See `CHANGELOG-v2.1.md`.*
